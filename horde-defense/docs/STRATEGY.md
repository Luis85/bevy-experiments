# Late-campaign strategic systems

`StrategyState` is saved with `Game`. It owns recruited survivors, guard assignments, plague alerts, a seeded random generator, a strategic tick clock, bounded explanatory history and the maximum number of simultaneously liberated countries seen so far. Historical unlocks therefore survive a country being reopened by plague pressure.

## Configuration and identity

`world.json` contains `strategy_config`: recruitment after 1 liberated country, parallel operations after 2, grand strategy after 4, up to 4 parallel deployments, 12 recruits, and 3 guards per country. A recruit costs `Campaign::recruit_price()` shared coins: the `recruit_cost` base of 100 scaled by the threat rank (100 / 110 / 200 / 300 / 600 at rank 0 / 1 / 10 / 20 / 50 and above, see PROGRESSION.md). These defaults are intentionally configurable rather than hard-coded progression gates in the UI.

Recruitment requires a currently liberated country and records that country as the survivor's origin. Each recruit has a persistent ID and name, a full independent `Npc` record, character-point awards/investments, loadout references, objectives, tactical policy and deployment history. Recruitment grants a genuinely new protected starter set with four new globally unique armory IDs. It never creates a second reference to an already deployed rifle as a substitute for ownership.

`Game::recruit_offer(country)` names the next recruit before it joins: its name, appearance, the origin trait of the country's biome (flavour only, no stat effect), the core price and two distinct archetypes, each with its starting policy and stats. The first archetype is the rotation every recruit has always joined as (Marksman, Guardian, Scout, Steadfast by recruit id); the second is one of the other three, keyed on the game seed, the next recruit id and the country with `hash::keyed` and the `RECRUIT_OFFER` tag. Nothing about the offer is saved, and recruiting never draws the strategy RNG, so outbreak rolls are unchanged. A blocked offer names its reason, in this order: the Recruitment node with its name and price, a country that is not liberated, a full roster, a defender out on the main defense, too few coins. `Command::RecruitAs { country, archetype }` recruits as one offered archetype (by name; another answers `ArchetypeNotOffered`); `Command::Recruit(country)` takes the first offer, exactly as before.

An idle survivor may equip owned items, train, deploy, join a garrison or defend the main run. A survivor has one `RecruitStatus` at a time: Ready (in camp), Guarding (a garrison), Deployed (on an expedition or a country operation) or Defending (holding the main run, "Recruit takeover" below); the labels are "Ready", "Guarding", "On expedition" and "Defending". Taking a deployment, guard or takeover role reserves that survivor's actual loadout in `Campaign::reserved_items`. Reservation rejects another concurrent use, and protects those items from sale and reassignment. Return or recall releases the reservation. The simulation layer owns the actual isolated parallel battle states and synchronizes only completed reward deltas into the shared campaign.

## Defenders and the next defense

Every defense has one defender, a `DefenderId`: `Player` (the chosen survivor), `Mara` or `Recruit(id)`. Attempts and reports save it as their `"defender"` seat; a missing seat means the mode owner (the player for Personal, Mara for Npc), so a save written before version 7 reads unchanged and the mode-owner digests do not move. `Attempt::defender()` and `Report::defender()` resolve it. The choice of who defends next is the only saved defender state: `Campaign.next_defender` (not written while None, always None in an expedition's worker game). `Game::next_defender()` is that choice, or else the defender of the last report, or else the player, so a journey keeps its defender until the player changes it.

Mara can be chosen once the Meet Mara node (`companion_contact`, the Companion feature) is owned, and recruits once Survivor network (`recruitment_network`, the Recruitment feature) is owned. The commands:

- `SetNextDefender(id)` stores the choice between defenses only. A worker game refuses it, a live defense answers `DefenseInProgress` and a recruit id not on the roster `UnknownSurvivor`. Otherwise the defender must be selectable, or the answer is `DefenderUnavailable { defender, blocker }` with one `DefenderBlocker`, checked in this order: `Locked { node_id }` (Mara before Meet Mara, a recruit before Survivor network); for a recruit, its duty, `Guarding { country_id }`, `OnExpedition { battle }` or `Defending`; then `InvalidLoadout { reason }`: a recruit loadout that fails slot validation or weapon access, or, for any defender (the player and Mara too), an item another deployment holds. Their player texts are "Locked until you unlock <node>", "Guarding <country>", "Away on an expedition", "Already defending the front" and "Cannot deploy: <reason>". A refusal changes nothing.
- `BeginDefense { stage }` deploys `next_defender()` on the active country's stage, unpauses like `Deploy` and clears the choice on success.
- `ContinueJourney` deploys `next_defender()` on the journey's next stage (the reported stage again after a loss) and clears the choice on success. A defender that cannot defend now is refused with nothing changed, and the pure `Game::continue_blocker()` gives the same error in advance. Retry and repeat redeploy the report's defender; a repeat still needs an Npc defender, which a recruit is.
- `Deploy { stage, mode }` keeps its recorded meaning: Personal is the player and Npc is Mara. It never reads or clears the choice.
- Sending the next defender away resets the choice. `AssignGuard`, `DeploySurvivor` and `CountryOperation` on the recruit `next_defender()` names still succeed; then the player defends next and the strategy log says "<name> left the next defense; you will defend next."

`Game::available_defenders()` lists the player, Mara (even while locked) and then the roster by id. Each `DefenderOption` carries the defender's card, `SetNextDefender`'s verdict as `Result<(), DefenderBlocker>`, whether it is the next defender, and its power at the next threat. `Game::defender_card(id)` gives the name, the HUD name (the first word in capitals, such as "ALEX"), the appearance (a recruit's comes from its identity, Mara's is Female), the role (Survivor, Companion or Recruit), the archetype, a recruit's origin (country and origin trait), the mode, and the level with its cap (the player's level and level cap; for Mara and recruits the advancement level and the campaign cap level, PROGRESSION.md "Recruit advancement allowance"). `active_defender()` is the card of the live main-run defender, `battle_defender_card(battle)` the card of an expedition's recruit read from this game's roster, and `defender_loadout(id)` the items a defender carries.

The frontend only reads these and dispatches commands. The defender picker on Results, the Briefing and Camp home's Next defense card lists the options as chips (a combo box beyond three), disables a blocked one with core's reason as its hover, lists Mara once Meet Mara is owned, and is hidden during a live defense or when there is only one defender to list. Camp home's Defenders list gives each defender's status, HP, level and power line and a "Defend next" button. The Briefing primary reads "Deploy personally" or "Deploy <name>" and dispatches `BeginDefense`.

## Recruit takeover

A Ready recruit can defend the main run in place of the player or Mara (rulings R2, D5, D6 and D8). It fights under the Npc rules with its own loadout, tactics, training and identity, at the campaign's threat rank like every main run; only expeditions follow the recruit (PROGRESSION_SCALING.md, "Expedition threat").

**Deploy.** `BeginDefense` or `ContinueJourney` with a recruit as the next defender (or `Game::deploy_country_stage_as`) first runs the preflight, which changes nothing: no worker game, the selection verdict above (unknown, locked, away, loadout, held elsewhere), then the campaign's preparation check. The commit reserves the recruit's loadout and the main run (`Campaign.reserved` Npc and `Campaign.reserved_recruit` the recruit's id) and moves the recruit from Ready to Defending (`StrategyState::reserve_defender`); if either step fails, both roll back. The attempt takes the recruit's loadout profile, the seat `Recruit(id)` and the recruit's identity appearance. A takeover reserves only the recruit's items, never Mara's idle loadout, so a sale check never reports Mara's equipment as deployed.

**During the defense.** The defending recruit's roster entry is the battle's npc: one saved copy, nothing copied back at the end. Its hits and combat seconds accrue there and claim its objectives against the campaign allowance. `SetRecruitTactics { id, tactics }` edits a Ready or Defending recruit's policy, aim, early reload, risk and recall threshold; a Defending recruit uses them from its next npc turn. A Guarding or Deployed recruit answers `RecruitNotReady`, and a recall threshold above `MAX_RECALL_PERCENT` answers `RecallThresholdOutOfRange`. `SetRecruitPolicy` stays Ready-only and `SetNpcPolicy` always edits Mara; the battle's Tactics popup edits whoever defends. `AssignGuard`, `DeploySurvivor`, `CountryOperation`, `TrainRecruit`, `EquipRecruit`, `BuyRecruitPoint` and `SetRecruitPolicy` on the Defending recruit answer `DefenderUnavailable { blocker: Defending }` and change nothing. The world tick claims no guard objectives for a Deployed or Defending recruit, and nobody is recruited while a main defense is live.

**Finish.** A takeover ends in this order. On a victory, the campaign is credited (`commit_victory`: the player's completion XP, the boss, the campaign first clear, one more victory and so one more threat rank, Mara's objective claims) and so is the recruit (`credit_npc_victory`: its victory, its first clear and its objective claims; Mara is not credited), then the country stage clear and the outbreak resolution follow. Then, whatever the outcome: one more attempt on the recruit; the takeover release (the items freed, `reserved_recruit` cleared, Defending back to Ready); the main-run auto-sell, judged for the recruit (PROGRESSION.md, "Auto-sell"); the level-up grant and rank milestones; and the run record (`recruit_id` the recruit, `worker_id` None, the recruit's name) and the report, which keeps the seat.

**Saves.** A live takeover saves and resumes mid-battle. Validation requires at the top level: a recruit seat only on an Npc attempt; with a live recruit seat, exactly that recruit Defending, `reserved` Npc, `reserved_recruit` its id, its loadout reserved and no expedition or guard holding it; with no attempt or a mode-owner seat, no Defending recruit and no `reserved_recruit`; a report's recruit seat only on an Npc report of a roster recruit; a `next_defender` recruit on the roster; and a run record that names a recruit without a worker only on an Npc run. A worker game holds no `reserved_recruit`, `next_defender` or Defending recruit.

## Expedition outcomes

Each expedition (`ParallelBattle`) keeps an `ExpeditionSummary` (ruling D10): the final `outcome`, `stages_cleared`, whether it `liberated` its country, up to 32 newly opened `fronts`, the `coins` and `loot_count` it banked, and `acknowledged`. Settlement writes it as each stage commits, so a country operation settled over several calls or across a save counts every stage once, and the outcome and fronts are filled in when the battle settles. Expeditions settled before save version 7 have no summary and are never announced.

`Game::unacknowledged_outcomes()` lists the settled, unacknowledged expeditions oldest first (battle, recruit, country and summary) with `folded`, the count pruning folded. `AcknowledgeOutcomes { battles, folded }` marks the named ones seen and takes `folded` off the count; unknown, live and already acknowledged ids are ignored and the count saturates at 0, so it is idempotent. Above 32 kept battles, pruning drops settled ones oldest first, a summary-less or acknowledged one before an unacknowledged one, whose outcome then joins `StrategyState.folded_outcomes` so none goes uncounted; live battles stay.

The frontend toasts each pending outcome once ("<Recruit> liberated <Country> · N new fronts", otherwise "<Recruit> held <Country>", "was driven back from" or "retreated from", with "+C coins" when it earned any) and queues `AcknowledgeOutcomes` in the frame that shows it; folded outcomes become one "N more expedition outcomes · Operations" toast. Toasts appear on Camp, Results, Upgrades and History (one at a time on a paused battle) and wait during a live unpaused defense; Camp home's "Recent reports" lists the newest lines. The main run's liberation notice and the core wording share `world::liberation_line` ("Belgium liberated · 2 new fronts open on the map.").

An expedition attempt carries the seat `Recruit(id)` and the recruit's identity appearance at both deploy sites (a single stage and an operation's next stage). Its victory credits the worker's copy of the recruit, which settlement returns to the roster when the expedition ends, and the run record names both `recruit_id` and `worker_id`.

## Defender power and service records

`Game::defender_power(defender, threat)` is a pure analytic reading (no RNG, no mutation) of the inputs a deploy uses (`Game::deployment_inputs`), in the defender's own model. Personal: kills per minute and survival seconds against the reference Shambler, combined into a rating as their geometric mean relative to a fresh starter's. Npc (W4-16, R62): the rating is the heaviest arrival load at which the defender still wins the stage, relative to a fresh Mara's; kills per minute is the turn-limited kill rate at mid-field against the stage's hostile mix and survival the seconds held against the stage's own arrivals. Either rating is exactly 100 for a fresh starter at threat 0, clamped to 1-999, in the band Weak (below 85), Even (85-115) or Strong (above 115). It reads the rank only, with no country modifiers or front ramp. `Game::next_threat()` is the campaign rank, and `available_defenders` reads power there, held at the highest supported threat; Camp home shows it as "Power P · threat T · Band". `Game::outbreak_plan(country).recommended_defender` is the selectable defender with the highest rating (the earlier listed on a tie, the player when nobody is selectable).

`Game::service_record(recruit)` derives a recruit's history on every read from the run records that name it: deployments, takeovers (records without a worker) and distinct expeditions, victories, defeats, withdrawals, kills, coins, distinct stages cleared, liberations (wins on a country's final stage), the countries fought in and the last outcome; and from the recruit itself: its attempt and victory counts, its live guard duty and its saved `containments`. When the recruit has more attempts than records (runs from before records named their recruit), `since_records_began()` is true and the Operations roster adds "(since records began)". An unknown recruit answers `UnknownSurvivor`.

## Plague pressure

Strategic time starts after the grand-strategy milestone and advances only when the caller advances the unpaused simulation. Closing the application creates no outbreaks or rewards. A default check occurs every 36,000 simulation ticks (ten minutes). A seeded candidate is chosen from currently liberated countries; the default chance is 35%, reduced by 30 percentage points per assigned guard. Each active alert has its own ID, start, deadline, country, remaining infected groups and authoritative enemy injury state.

The default response window is 25,200 ticks (seven minutes), long enough to complete a fresh defense including its boss. An actual successful country deployment may contain that country's active alert. A garrison fights its alert through the procedure below. An alert that reaches its deadline reopens only the country's final stage. Existing travel unlocks persist. Neither alerts nor containment award free combat coins. The alert outcome is recorded as Active, Contained or Overrun, so resolving a completed alert again cannot pay or reopen it again.

## Guard combat adapter

Guards remain persistent survivors with their real equipment and training. The strategic resolver is an explicit **scenario adapter** for a garrison skirmish, separate from the deployment-frozen encounter renderer. It uses one-second action opportunities, the same 3d6 attack/active-defense/protection/injury module, the guard's Guns skill, rifle damage and ammunition, outfit/accessory protection, HT checks, Dodge, stun, posture, readiness and unconsciousness/lethal outcomes. The current infected group uses an authored close-engagement scenario profile: 9 HP, HT 10, attack 9, Dodge 7, 1d crushing damage and no armor. A seeded alert contains three to seven groups. These values are original scenario content.

Ammunition and multi-turn reloads are real state. A critical result requiring readiness consumes a separate Ready turn and cannot refill the magazine. Shock expires at the next action boundary, even if the survivor spends that turn recovering from stun or changing posture. Defeating a group advances the alert once; defeating the last contains it. Each containment adds one to the saved `Recruit.containments` counter (W2-21): of the guard whose attack defeats the last group, or of every guard of the country when the last group falls to its own wounds. The counter lives on the recruit rather than the guard assignment, so it outlasts a recall; it saturates, defaults to 0 and is not written while 0, so older saves load with 0. `Game::service_record` reports it. Incapacitated guards release their role, the alert remains actionable, and the campaign recovery provision restores the survivor between assignments.

Ordinary post-battle exertion costs one FP after a garrison fight lasting more than ten simulated seconds. It does not charge FP every shot or every ten seconds. Guard FP and injury persist until recalled; recall applies the baseline campaign recovery provision. Multiple guards receive separate actions, while the infected group receives only one action per country each second. Reloading, readiness, stun recovery and posture changes do not prevent that hostile action.

## Verification boundary

Strategy tests exercise independent equipment identities, guard/deployment exclusivity, reservation release, deterministic strategic save/serialization continuation, manual containment idempotency and outbreak expiry without free currency. The larger simulation suite owns parallel reward merging, global pause, save/resume and battle lifecycle evidence. Test helper campaigns construct liberated states directly; they are not evidence that all preceding country stages have been beaten by a human player.


## Readiness, sales and individual advancement

`StrategyState::readiness(campaign, id)` derives an individual survivor's role, prepared weapon/skill/protection profile and current guard HP/FP. It does not invent an expedition success percentage. Parallel battle injuries remain owned by that battle's live attempt. New recruits have equal 76-point creation budgets with different attributes, skills and initial policies; see PROGRESSION.md. Free redistribution restores the selected recruit's own creation baseline.

Sales are strict since ruleset 2 (W1-20): equipment an idle recruit wears cannot be sold, and the refusal names that survivor ("Equipped by <name>"); a blocked sale for a preset names the presets. The same rule covers single and batch sales, and also excludes protected, locked, favorite, reserved and preset-referenced equipment and the player's and Mara's loadouts. Active deployment or garrison reservations remain unsellable. A sale never rewrites a loadout, so no sale needs roster reconciliation; loading a save still runs `StrategyState::reconcile_loadouts`, which returns an idle survivor slot whose item is no longer owned to that survivor's genuinely owned protected starter set without granting a duplicate.

Guard objective progress now claims finite earned character points at the next strategic update, including pending awards when a milestone opens capacity. Deployed survivors keep their battle-owned award ledger until settlement. Paid training never substitutes for an already earned objective. Strategic save validation checks individual creation/advancement, unique guard equipment, actual magazine bounds, valid injury states, active alert identities and countries, deadlines, and independently bounded histories.


Guardian policy now uses its trained melee weapon in the close-engagement garrison scenario; other guards may use melee when a magazine is empty and fatigue permits it. This resolves through the actual melee skill, weapon damage type and ST-adjusted swing profile. It does not consume firearm ammunition and does not count toward the ranged-hit objective. Reloads already in progress still consume their legal action opportunities. Low FP applies the same supported strength reduction to muscle-powered damage, while firearm damage stays unchanged.
