# Player guidance and continuing goals

The Activities inspector is a continuing view of the player's actual world.
It does not add tutorial inventory, an alternative permission model or a second
completion ledger. Every suggestion is derived from the state that ordinary
commands already validate. Saved games therefore resume guidance from their
existing jobs, goods, structures, territory and progression.

## Current readiness and reviewed intent

The 0.23 supply query shares acceptance, delivery and return preparation with
authority. Readiness explains ownership, expiry, carried goods, receiving data,
recipient/wallet capacity and reach. A ready inspection does not reserve future
success; the command prepares again against current state. Missing receiving
data in a partial replica does not establish permanent whole-world absence.
Blocked delivery retains goods and reserved reward and names a recovery step.
Expired unclaimed offers are not advertised as acceptable before maintenance.

Acquisition checks complete bounded inventory exchange, including all recipe
outputs and space freed by consumed inputs. It can choose a smaller feasible
batch or a real alternative. Inactive owners receive a resume instruction with
no action. Native action activation retains the displayed person, resource,
territory or request; intervening context changes require renewed review rather
than silently changing targets. Selection and the active consuming person remain
distinct. No saved quest ledger, new wire transaction or price reservation is added.

## Useful public work and operational continuity

Version 0.24 publishes only useful amounts that fit the receiver, from 1 through 12
items. Existing public offers retain their terms while demand remains positive;
a temporary full receiver alone does not cancel them. Confirmed obsolete public
work retires only after exact original-issuer refund. A capped issuer retains
its escrow until normal spending permits recovery. Missing receiving data is
unknown, not deletion. Accepted promises remain deliverable on their original
terms even if the original shortage has since been filled.

Industry and Fleet retain the displayed operation and selected IDs through
activation and dispatch. A missing explicit selection requires deliberate
browsing; no first-loaded replacement is chosen. Building damage names the exact
structure and health effect, offers Cancel/Confirm, and rechecks current authority
before mutation. Stable operands are retained without freezing changing timers,
payroll or normal movement.

## Player workflows

| Goal | Derived next steps | Existing authority used |
|---|---|---|
| Suggested journey | Address critical water, food or energy; resume accepted road or supply work; continue an owned company; otherwise start supply work, establish a community and obtain a home | Active person's needs or registered driver's avatar, real supplies, current assignments, owner-private company state, completed supply work, accessible territory and housing |
| Supply work | Accept a funded request, acquire its exact goods, travel into service range, deliver | Contract ownership, finite issuer escrow, player inventory and interaction reach |
| A place to call home | Find or claim permitted land, obtain the real blueprint ingredients, place a house, set an accessible home | Catalog costs, plot permissions, placement checks and housing rules |
| Grow a community | Obtain founding goods and public treasury contribution, choose a name, earn XP, add housing/farms and contribute to the next charter | Settlement rules, personal/faction access, earned progression and finite contributions |
| Make a living on the road | Open the actual accepted job and assigned vehicle; otherwise choose suitable work or continue into the company tools | Vehicle ownership, qualifications, job status, physical travel and transport economy |
| Run your transport company | Register, fund, hire, compare a route, assign a vehicle and manage its current service; prioritize blocked loaded commitments over empty waiting services | Actual company ownership, cash, driver/route assignment, fleet status, physical load and reserved payment |

**Goal → choose** opens six direct choices in the existing Activities inspector;
choosing one closes the list and reveals its next step at the top of the inspector.
**Suggested** continues to follow urgent needs and
accepted work. An explicit goal stays selected for the session, without cancelling
another obligation. On reopening a world, Suggested rebuilds the next step from
saved authority; the UI choice is not a saved quest ledger. The current action
appears before long explanatory text, and the source banner keeps the next goal
visible when immersive controls hide the inspector. Step counts describe the
current workflow; they do not suggest that every play style has one linear ending.

### Returning to accepted work

**Your commitments** shows the personal wallet, up to three accepted supply
requests, accepted road-job count, owned-company cash/assigned drivers/loaded
commitments, and owned rental bonds or expiry. Company cash and rental bonds are
labelled separately from spendable personal money. A partial view is identified
as the current received view. Another player's requests, company or rental
obligations are not included.

Accepted supply requests are ordered by earliest world-clock expiry, with a stable
request-ID tie break. Without an explicitly selected accepted request, the Supply
guide uses that same priority. Selecting another accepted request preserves that
choice; choosing a goal does not silently replace it. **Review supply request #…**
opens the exact request named on the button and cycles through the retained
accepted list on subsequent reviewed choices. It never accepts, moves, delivers
or cancels. A successful review names the opened request in the status message and
reveals its next-step control. Automatic guidance updates and rejected stale
reviews preserve the reader's scroll position. Other panels keep their own place.
The next-step button remains a separate action.

The review action retains the displayed principal, restore revision and complete
request. If a request disappears, changes owner, reward, deadline, destination or
quantity, the old action refreshes without opening a replacement. Supply guidance
also retains that request identity even if two obligations suggest the same gather
command. The ordinary noticeboard's accept, deliver, return and travel controls
check the exact displayed request before dispatch. A restore or fallback to a
different request cannot turn an old click into a new transaction or destination.

### Supply costs and deadlines

Supply acceptance explains its actual deadline reset. The acceptance, acquisition
and ready-to-travel steps retain the **gross** reserved reward and community XP.
Missing-input guidance presents one comparison for the full missing quantity with
a visible seller that stocks it, using current listed personal-market prices.
The cheapest complete visible comparison wins, then distance and seller ID. A
cheaper partial stock does not imply that all required goods are available there.
If those inputs alone exceed the reward, the excess is explicit. An insufficient
wallet and its shortfall appear together; sufficient cash need not repeat the
commitments briefing. If no complete-quantity price comparison is available,
gathering, crafting or checking another region remain alternatives.

The acquisition instruction separately retains its actual seller and item. It
may suggest a nearer partially stocked market or an ingredient needed by a recipe;
neither is silently replaced by the full-quantity comparison seller. Recipe
prerequisites and one-unit affordability or exchange-capacity blockers stay visible.
Supply omits the repeated unit-price/stock/wallet paragraph; other journeys retain
their ordinary acquisition detail.

This comparison does not reserve stock, prices or money. Market still validates
the actual quoted exchange, including reach, quantity, funds and receiving
capacity. A stocked comparison is not proof that the full transaction can proceed.
It does not price already-carried goods, travel or the player's time and
does not promise profit. Starting goods are existing inventory, not a free cost
basis invented by the guide.

Deadline labels use world hours, then minutes and seconds near expiry. They follow
the calendar clock rather than elapsed wall time. A temporarily retained expired
accepted request offers an explicit no-reward return instead of an impossible
delivery; it consumes no carried goods. Normal settlement maintenance releases
expired claims, then evaluates public demand. Still-needed requests renew under
the same ID; confirmed obsolete requests retire only after exact, cap-safe issuer
credit. Unknown demand or a blocked refund retains escrow. Neither path adds a
late-delivery reward or deletes the player's goods.

Starting stock is ordinary world inventory. If the player already carries a
request's goods, the guide can move directly from acceptance to travel. It still
explains that delivery consumes those goods and transfers the reserved payment.
The next goal changes only after the command succeeds.

Suggested journey puts needs at **20% or below** ahead of work, in water, food,
then energy order. On foot, it offers the existing consume command for carried
water, bread, cooked fish or berries. Missing supplies use the same bounded
resource, recipe and market acquisition as other goals. Energy guidance opens
Command with the active owned person selected so the player can use the existing
Rest units controls. It does not enqueue a rest order when opening the panel.

When food is missing, the guide compares the supported foods actually available
in the current catalog and view. Ready gathering or crafting wins over a market
visit or travel, and a directly available food wins over preparing another
food's ingredients. Blocked recipes and unaffordable or capped sellers cannot
hide accessible berries or another stocked meal. This is bounded local guidance,
not a promise of the cheapest global route.

For a registered active vehicle, needs belong to the account's walking avatar,
matching the driver shown in Driving. The suggestion says to park and exit to
drink, eat or rest and opens Driving; the player still stops and exits through
ordinary controls. The guide never consumes supplies for a vehicle or exits it
automatically. Explicit Supply, Home, Community, Road and Company tracks keep the chosen
goal when needs are low. Recovery suggestions create no supplies, reward or
completion state, and existing work remains accepted.

## Company decisions after the first jobs

**Run your transport company** is a continuing goal, including for worlds saved
before it was added. Suggested journey selects it when the player owns a company,
after critical needs and accepted personal road/supply obligations. Explicit
tracks retain their chosen purpose. No new completion ledger or saved goal field
is needed: the briefing reads the current owner-private company state.

Before dispatch, the next step follows real setup: registration, funds, recruitment,
route comparison and vehicle assignment. Once a service is assigned, the briefing
names its driver, route, next stop or return pickup, completed paid deliveries and
company cash. It prioritizes a service needing attention, with loaded commitments
before empty waiting services and a stable driver-ID tie break. An idle spare
employee cannot hide an assigned vehicle's problem.

The action opens the specific driver, vehicle, route and relevant Fleet section.
Fuel/repair, held loads and blocked routes open Recovery; missing company cash or
public funding opens Company; finite stock, queues, demand or buyer funding opens
the relevant route/depot controls. An expired rental opens that exact vehicle in
Garage. Rental renewal can be paid personally from anywhere; registration and
recruitment still require being at a garage. Depositing company cash has no garage
proximity requirement.

The explanation distinguishes **Pause safely**, which finishes a loaded commitment,
from **Hold in place**, which stops immediately while keeping the load. It states
when payroll stops, when rental time continues, and why physical returns pay no
delivery reward. It never resumes, pays, orders a return or cancels work merely by
opening a panel. Recovery still uses ordinary stopped/reach, terrain, fuel,
condition, ownership, capacity, reservation and price checks. Empty pickup-capacity
failures are distinguished from loaded delivery and return failures. A visible
company payout limit directs the owner to review withdrawal capacity, while a full
employee wage wallet does not promise that another company deposit will help.
Accounting exhaustion is reported as a limit needing diagnosis; the guide does
not reset a ledger.

`GuideAction::OpenCompany(GuideCompanyFocus)` retains the owner, section, driver,
vehicle, route, next stop, facility, running/status and loaded-trip identity. A
changed assignment, hold, return or destination makes the old displayed action
stale and requires review. Routine vehicle movement or payroll accrual does not
invalidate a read-only handoff. The native cache also binds the displayed player
and restore revision, so a restored world with coincidentally matching IDs still
requires a fresh review if the earlier action was retained. The native handoff clears unrelated confirmations;
the rental handoff also clears old sale/return confirmations. Missing vehicle
bodies or facilities are described as outside the current view, not destroyed.

The Road track leads into the existing Fleet tools. **Routes** and **Drivers →
Before dispatch** now explain the selected route's first departure using its real
load, visible stock/queue and current issuer or buyer price. A warehouse route
also compares a named stocked retailer's price with the buyer's payment, showing
a merchandise shortfall before the player buys into a retail-to-wholesale loss.
An internal bay transfer says that it earns no payment.

Gross receipt divided by payroll rate is only a time-coverage bound before fuel,
inputs, return travel, setup and vehicle costs. Finite service/buyer funding,
reserved demand and the current view can still prevent loading. Unknown
contributed-input costs and management time remain explicit. Route creation,
stock purchase and dispatch continue through existing commands; inspecting or
changing a draft grants no cash, goods or progress. **Finances** supplies the
actual cash history after operating. See [COMPANIES.md](COMPANIES.md) for the
calculation, limits and finite-career evidence.

This connects guidance to a repeatable business decision without inventing a
separate career ledger. It does not supply an authored quest library, global
economic forecast or evidence about how long human players stay engaged.

## SDK contract

`SandboxState::activity_guidance(player_id, catalog, track, preferred_contract)`
returns an `ActivityGuidance`: title, detailed reason, current step, action label
and an optional `GuideAction`. The function is independent of Bevy and does not
mutate the state.

The reference UI ordinarily refreshes this inexpensive interpretation at most five
times per real second, with immediate refresh after a principal change or restore.
On activation it checks the displayed principal/restore revision, derives the
current suggestion again and calls
`ActivityGuidance::same_intent` against the displayed guidance, including any
retained supply request, and `GuideAction::same_intent` for the action itself. If the transaction or
destination has changed, the UI updates its explanation and asks the player to
review it. A click on a displayed acceptance cannot silently become a different
command. The ordinary command queue and authority perform their normal
validation afterwards, including on multiplayer hosts.

`SandboxState::activity_commitments(player_id)` returns the read-only owner
briefing. Supply rows are capped by the ordinary three-active-request limit;
company, road and rental information uses aggregate counts. It requires an
existing character and living account and adds no persistent field. The native
UI refreshes it alongside the existing five-per-real-second guidance cache.

`GuideAction::OpenDriving { job_id, vehicle_id }` identifies the actual accepted
assignment. The native UI sets both selections, clears unrelated garage/course
navigation and opens Driving. It does not infer the assignment from an offer
that the player happened to browse earlier.

`GuideAction::OpenMarket { shop_id }` preserves the actual seller named by the
guide, even when another shop is closer. A changed seller is a changed intent.
The guide opens this market rather than buying automatically. Personal Market
buy/sell actions retain the displayed seller, item, quantity and total; the
quoted command rejects a changed price before moving money or goods. The
read-only `SandboxState::personal_trade_quote` applies the same reach, stock,
funds and receiving-capacity checks as execution. Legacy `LivingCommand::Buy`
and `Sell` remain current-price APIs for embedding games; the native Market uses
`TradeQuoted` for an explicit price expectation.

## Acquisition and permissions

The guide first accounts for goods already carried. It considers accessible,
nondepleted resources and affordable stocked sellers, including a seller of a
finished crafted item before requiring its ingredients. Catalog recipes and
their real quantities drive crafting suggestions. Recipe recursion is bounded
to prevent custom cyclic catalogs from causing a loop.

Holding every recipe ingredient does not imply that processing is available:
missing or distant workbenches still allow a stocked finished-item seller to
be considered before asking the player to build or visit a workbench.

Resource permission work is restricted to the nearest 128 candidates per item.
Urgent food may compare three supported foods and their bounded recipe paths.
Selection
uses a linear partition and a small sort, with a lazily built spatial index of
inaccessible plots. Moving or changing the view refreshes the shortlist. The
message states the bounded search when no permitted resource is found; it does
not claim that no such resource exists anywhere. Territory and faction rules
remain authoritative, and a partial multiplayer/regional view is never treated
as proof that distant infrastructure is absent.

## Physical travel and failures

The travel action orders the active owned actor through `MoveUnits`; it does
not change its coordinates directly. The reference chooses a stopping point
outside known structure and facility footprints, within interaction range of
the service. It checks source coverage, dry terrain and occupied space. For a
vehicle, the final approach also requires the shared wheel/body support contract.
An unloaded or blocked approach produces an actionable explanation. Large
vehicles that cannot fit within service range require selecting a person.

These are bounded approach and route checks, not a global pathfinding guarantee.
Crowds, later obstruction, changed permissions, depleted stock or new offers can
change what the player can do. The guide refreshes, while command rejection
preserves inventory and money. Opening another goal does not cancel an existing
contract or transport obligation.

## Verification and extension boundary

The domain regressions cover read-only derivation, real contract acceptance and
exactly-once delivery payment, owner filtering, protected/depleted resources,
actual recipe costs, cyclic catalogs, affordable finished goods, home/founding
permissions, stale intent, assignment identity, bounded search and save/resume.
Urgent-needs checks cover water priority, real consumption and acquisition,
driver-avatar selection, exit guidance, explicit-track preservation and read-only
derivation. Food alternatives, workbench recovery, overlapping markets and
changed seller identity are covered. Quoted personal trades cover exact totals,
stale buy/sell rejection, read-only preflight, stock/funds/capacity failures,
three-dimensional reach and legacy command compatibility. Native integration
verifies that the rest prompt selects the active person and opens Command
without issuing an order.
Native tests exercise action integration, physical service approach and layout.
The 0.16 commitment regressions also cover earliest-deadline ordering, explicit
choice retention, owner filtering, complete-quantity price comparison, clock
labels, stale request context and read-only direct goal selection. A repeatable
continuity case accepts three funded requests through ordinary commands, saves
and resumes, returns one, misses one deadline, physically delivers the remaining
wood request and rejects duplicate return/payment. Its report identifies authored
flat SDK support, ordinary starting goods and separately advanced calendar and
physical time. It is a regression scenario, not a human comprehension study or a
career-profitability result. Native review must separately inspect both normal
and enlarged-text Activities layouts and the save/reopen handoff.
Company-guidance regressions follow ordinary paid setup, a loaded hold and exact
JSON restoration, source-blocked recovery, urgent/accepted-work priority, owner
privacy, stale service identity, remote rental renewal, money/capacity limits and
legacy saves without an accounting history. Native integration verifies exact
Recovery and Garage handoffs, stale-action/restore refresh and no commands or state
changes from opening them. A prepared-source journey physically approaches a garage,
uses ordinary paid setup from the starting wallet, dispatches to an actual passenger
pickup and holds the loaded service. An optional `TERRA_COMPANY_REVIEW_OUTPUT` points
that test at a new private folder for native-review saves; generated worlds are not
source fixtures or proof of an uncoached player journey.
The release record identifies the actual rendered journey and final test count.

Games can add tracks or their own UI without persisting this derived view. Any
new action should use the existing command boundary, describe its real cost and
maintain the displayed-intent check. Long career pacing, localization, an authored
quest/content library and player-study evidence remain game development work.
